Personal Project · Team of 2 · Jun 2026 – Present

KBO Games — 3 Browser Quiz Games on KBO Player Data

kbogames.com · Game Design · Feature Spec · QA (AI-paired programming)

Background

Inspired by the popular football fan guessing games "Who Are Ya" and "Missing XI," I wanted to bring the same experience to fans of the KBO (Korea Baseball Organization). Built as a 2-person team with a friend, this is a real, shipped web product I designed and helped build end to end.

Games (3)

  • WHO ARE YA — A Wordle-style guessing game. Guessing a player's name compares 6 attributes (team, position, age with up/down indicators, throw/bat hand, nationality) with color-coded feedback. Includes autocomplete, 3 difficulty tiers, win/loss popups, and team logo badges.
  • MISSING 9 — Guess all 9 starting-lineup players for a given team, position by position. Wrong guesses progressively reveal hints — throwing hand, batting hand, then birth year.
  • CAREER PATH — Guess a player's identity from their team-history timeline alone, with 3 attempts.

What I did

  • Led game design, feature specification, and QA, working in an AI-paired-programming workflow with Claude to implement features, fix bugs, troubleshoot deployment, and verify data accuracy
  • Processed real KBO batting/pitching stat CSVs with Python scripts to build custom datasets: 3,486 all-time players, 718 WAR5+ players, 433 WAR10+ players, and 433 career-path records
  • Cross-referenced the 184-player active roster against multiple sources (team rosters, transfer/retirement news) to correct outdated affiliations, fix name-collision mismatches, and remove confirmed retirees
  • Designed a copyright-safe logo component that falls back to an initials badge when no official team logo asset is available
  • Hands-on production deployment troubleshooting, including diagnosing and fixing a Vercel build failure caused by an incorrect module import path

Results

Built with Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, and Tailwind CSS 4, and shipped live on kbogames.com with version control on GitHub. Iteratively improved UI/UX issues (mismatched result-cell coloring, inconsistent popups) through AI-paired development, gaining hands-on experience in real-world collaborative software delivery.

EST 389 · Spring 2026 · Team of 4

How Much Does Qualifying Position Determine Race Outcomes in Formula 1?

Open API-based F1 data analysis & hypothesis validation · Planning & Analysis

Research Questions

  • How does qualifying position affect the probability of winning a race?
  • How do Safety Car and Red Flag events affect position changes during a race?
  • Does the effect of qualifying position vary across circuits?
  • Has the importance of qualifying position changed across recent seasons?

What I did

  • Collected raw F1 race data from the modern hybrid era (2014–2024) via open APIs, writing the extraction and cleaning code to build the analysis pipeline
  • Validated statistical correlations between variables through multi-dimensional analysis — overall, per-circuit, and per-season

Key Findings

Qualifying position is a strong structural determinant of race outcomes — particularly winning — but its effect varies substantially across circuits and can be partially disrupted by Safety Car events. The project demonstrated the ability to move beyond data extraction to meaningful strategic conclusions.

EST 441 · Spring 2026 · Individual Research

How Does AI's Automation Technology Affect Employment in the Software Engineering Field?

Analysis of AI automation's impact on the software engineering job market · Sole author

Motivation

Returning to a campus where AI tools had quietly become the norm, I realized the software engineering labor market I was about to enter had fundamentally changed within just a few years. I hypothesized that AI automation's employment effects are not uniform — they differ by developers' experience level, expertise, and type of work.

What I did

  • Collected and analyzed labor market indicators, productivity data, and occupational experience data for 2023–2026 (secondary data from Stanford HAI, Stack Overflow, and others)
  • Structurally analyzed the differential impact on junior vs. senior developers, the emergence of new AI job categories, and the magnitude of wage gaps

Key Findings

  • Junior developer tasks are being rapidly replaced by AI tools, with entry-level hiring declining sharply
  • Highly experienced engineers gain substantial productivity from AI, increasing their value to employers
  • An entirely new category of AI-specialist roles has emerged, deepening wage polarization
